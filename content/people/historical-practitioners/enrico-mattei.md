Italian Partisan commander during the Second World War, later commissioner of the state petroleum agency AGIP and founding president of ENI (Ente Nazionale Idrocarburi) from 1953 until his death in 1962. Mattei was appointed to AGIP after the war with instructions to liquidate the Fascist-era agency. Instead he expanded exploration in the Po Valley, discovered commercial natural gas at Caviaga in 1946 and Cortemaggiore in 1949, and built the pipeline infrastructure that supplied Italian industry with cheap domestic energy through the post-war economic miracle. Italians called ENI "a state within the state."

Mattei's international strategy is what earned him the [[glossary/retention-economics|retention]]-lineage entry. His [[mattei-formula|Mattei Formula]] offered oil-producing host countries a 75-25 profit split in their favor (the host country taking 50 percent, then optionally sharing production costs to take half the remaining 50 percent), against the standard 50-50 split offered by the Anglo-American major oil companies known as the Seven Sisters. His 1957 deal with Iran and his oil deals with the Soviet Union antagonized the majors directly. Declassified United States National Security Council documents from the Eisenhower administration record that Mattei was viewed as "a thorn in the side" of American oil interests.

Mattei died in a plane crash near Bascapè in the province of Pavia on 27 October 1962, along with his pilot Irnerio Bertuzzi and the American journalist William McHale. In 1995 his body was exhumed. A 1997 reopened investigation and forensic examination found evidence consistent with an explosive device. In 2003 a Pavia magistrate's investigation concluded that the crash had been a criminal act of sabotage, though no perpetrators were ever identified. This wiki reports only the court and forensic record and does not speculate on responsibility. Mattei belongs on this list as the paradigm case of retention succeeding economically (Italian energy sovereignty underwrote the miracle) while running fatally against the dominant Anglo-American oil order.
