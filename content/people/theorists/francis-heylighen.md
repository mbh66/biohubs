Belgian cyberneticist at the Free University of Brussels, director of the Global Brain Institute, and the researcher who extended [[glossary/stigmergy|stigmergy]] from its original entomological setting to a general theory of self-organizing human coordination. His 2016 paper *Stigmergy as a universal coordination mechanism* set out the framework used to analyze Wikipedia, open-source software, and other online commons as stigmergic systems.

Heylighen's work matters to the BioConomy because it names the mechanism through which distributed human coordination can operate at scale without central command, which is the operating question the [[glossary/e-form-emergent|E form]] and [[glossary/mycelial-coordination|Mycelial Coordination]] both address.
