German-American economist and journalist, exiled from Württemberg in 1825, resident in the United States from 1825 to 1832, and the intellectual bridge between Hamilton's American System and the German industrial takeoff under Bismarck. List directly studied the American System during his years in Pennsylvania and drew from it his conviction that economic doctrine had to be indexed to a nation's stage of development. His *Outlines of American Political Economy* (1827) previewed the framework he elaborated in *Das nationale System der politischen Ökonomie* (*The National System of Political Economy*, 1841).

List's core argument was that backward countries cannot develop new industries in the presence of more advanced competitors without state intervention, especially tariff protection of infant industries, until they are strong enough to compete on their own. He advocated abolishing Germany's internal tariffs, an aim achieved through the Zollverein customs union, while protecting against foreign, especially British, competition. His framework was explicitly stage-based: protection was a temporary developmental phase, and free trade was appropriate once a nation reached the industrial frontier. This is still growth-phase logic and marks a limit the historical retention tradition never crossed.

List died by suicide on 30 November 1846, impoverished and marginalized. His ideas were widely republished in Germany over the following decades and, according to the *Times* Berlin correspondent cited in the Nicholson edition of the *National System*, exercised powerful influence toward a protective commercial policy. German industrial output roughly doubled by 1913. By 1900 German industry was second in the world after the United States, dominant in chemicals and electricals. List is the second paradigm case of [[structural-prematurity|Structural Prematurity]] in the retention lineage: a diagnosis correct enough to be vindicated by Bismarck's Germany, offered a generation too early to be implemented, and destroying its author in the interval.
