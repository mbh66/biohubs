Morris, C.E., et al. (2014). "Bioprecipitation: a feedback cycle linking Earth history, ecosystem dynamics and land use through biological ice nucleators in the atmosphere." *Global Change Biology*, 20(2): 341-351.

The primary reference for the bioprecipitation hypothesis: that intact vegetation contributes to local rainfall through biological mechanisms including biogenic volatile organic compounds seeding cloud formation, ice-nucleation-active bacteria catalyzing precipitation, and mesoscale moisture recirculation. The hypothesis is scientifically coherent and represents a legitimate research frontier; it is not, however, established science in most biomes, and [[glossary/biohub|BioHub]] practitioners are advised to keep it separate from the defensible operational claim that coordinated landscape restoration co-produces usable water yield.
