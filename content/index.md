This wiki documents the [[bioconomy|BioConomy]] framework: a coordination architecture for economies organized around [[bioregion|BioRegions]].

### Two Phases of a Growth Curve

Every living system follows an S-curve: slow growth at first, then rapid, then slow. The human body grows slowly as an embryo, rapidly as a teenager, and slows down during adulthood. The same pattern repeats in every living system, including the economic system every society depends on. 

The steep middle of the economic S-curve rewards [[throughput]]: rapid extraction, capital mobility, and expansion into new territory. Growth is the point, and the mechanisms that maximize flow (trade volume, GDP, return on capital) are structurally fit for that phase. This is how we've used the phrase "[[economy]]" since Smith and Ricardo. It served its purpose, and it built the industrial world.

But the growth curve doesn't remain steep forever. As a system matures, the logic that suited acceleration begins to work against it. Extraction against limits degrades the capacity to reproduce. Maximizing flow drains the substrate that made flow possible. A mature system requires a different measure: value counted by what stays in place: soil, water, biodiversity, skill, community capacity, and cultural coherence. This is BioConomy.
