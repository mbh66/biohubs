A funding architecture in which capital enters the commons substrate and becomes the substrate, carrying three features found in every historical patronage form that endured: constitutive investment, embedding in the emerging order, and a replication mechanism that survives the founder.

## Extended definition

Historical patronage has produced two kinds of outcomes. The rare successes (the Benedictine network, the Cistercian order, the Medici Platonic revival, the Jesuits, the Hanseatic League) built architectures that survived their founders. The many failures (Roger Bacon, Francis Bacon, the Fuggers, the EA/FTX network, captured NGOs, the late Roman senatorial aristocracy) produced brilliant documents, decorative philanthropy, or absorbed reform organizations. The Three-Feature Test identifies what separates the two.

Mycelial Patronage inherits the three features but changes the structural relationship between patron and reform. In every historical case the relationship was hierarchical: a patron above, a project below, capital flowing downward. In Mycelial Patronage, the patron's capital enters the substrate and becomes the substrate. The commons trust holds it in perpetuity. The bioregional currency circulates it. The patron participates as a community member with standing earned through contribution, not through capital control.

## Contrast with adjacent terms

Mycelial Patronage is not regenerative capitalism (which retains private ownership and asks it to serve regeneration). It is not impact investment (which retains the option of exit). It is not philanthropy (which is retractable on the funder's schedule). The Patagonia transfer to the Patagonia Purpose Trust and the Holdfast Collective in 2022 is the closest contemporary precedent; the Bosch and Zeiss/Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung models are historical analogues.
