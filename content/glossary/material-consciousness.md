The form of consciousness that privileges analytical, perspectival, individual intelligence applied to commodified labor, corresponding to Gebser's mental-rational structure, Graves's First Tier value systems, and Kegan's self-authoring mind.

## Extended Definition

Material Consciousness is the BioConomy corpus's term for the form of awareness that has governed the last several centuries of civilization and produced its characteristic achievements: science, individual rights, market economics, industrial technology. It is the fourth stage in the [[concepts/major-evolutionary-transitions|Major Evolutionary Transitions]] sequence.

The defining capacity of Material Consciousness is perspective: the ability to stand outside nature, outside tradition, and outside the group, to analyze, abstract, and measure. This capacity makes possible the commodification of land, labor, and money that Karl Polanyi identified as the structural foundation of the market economy.

Under Material Consciousness, Steiner's V2 operates as the application of individual intelligence to commodified labor, producing productivity gains captured as private capital. This is not a failure of the system. It is the system operating as designed under this form of consciousness.

## Contrast with Adjacent Terms

Material Consciousness is not a pejorative. It describes a specific developmental achievement with specific structural consequences. The BioConomy corpus does not argue that Material Consciousness is wrong or bad. It argues that it is incomplete, that it describes only one mode of value creation, and that the conditions now emerging (S-curve deceleration, ecological overshoot, complexity collapse) require a form of consciousness adequate to distributed, ecological coordination, which Material Consciousness cannot provide.

Material Consciousness is to [[glossary/mycelial-consciousness|Mycelial Consciousness]] as a young, competitive ecosystem is to a mature, cooperative one in Sahtouris's biological framework.
