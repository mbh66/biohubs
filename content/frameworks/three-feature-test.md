The Three-Feature Test is the master diagnostic for evaluating whether a patron project meets the structural conditions historically associated with durable civilizational reform. It is derived from a cross-cluster analysis of successful and failed patronage cases across roughly 1,500 years of documented history.

## Overview

The test rests on a single empirical finding: every durable case of civilizational reform patronage in the historical record carries three specific features, and every failure is missing at least one of them. The three features are constitutive investment, embedding in the emerging order, and protection during maximum exposure. Funding volume, personal integrity, and stated intent are not predictive of durability. The three features are.

The test is applied to any patron project by asking three questions, each with a specific structural criterion. A project that passes all three has the structural conditions historically associated with durability. A project that fails one or more has a specific, named vulnerability that the test surfaces.

The test is developed at length in the [[research/historical-patronage-three-feature-test|associated research brief]], which treats the historical cases from which the test is derived. This page states the test itself and provides the questions and criteria in usable form.

## The three features

### 1. Constitutive investment

**The test:** Would the patron's position in the emerging order collapse if the reform failed?

If yes, the investment is constitutive. If the patron can walk away and retain their standing, the investment is instrumental.

**Positive anchors (constitutive):**
- The Medici: their cultural authority existed only because they built the Platonic revival. No revival, no prestige system independent of banking.
- Charlemagne: his empire ran on written capitularies. Without literate monks, the administrative apparatus did not function.
- Nizam al-Mulk: the Nizamiyya madrasa network was the Seljuk state's ideological and administrative backbone. The state's legitimacy depended on the network's success.
- Sophie Charlotte and Frederick I of Brandenburg-Prussia: the Berlin Academy was part of the positional upgrade of Berlin into a royal capital for Frederick's 1701 coronation.

**Negative anchors (instrumental):**
- The Fuggers: patronized humanists while their core business (indulgence collection, Habsburg finance) was structurally committed to the order Luther attacked. Cultural patronage was portfolio decoration.
- EA/FTX: the return structure was reputational capital, moral positioning, and intellectual community. Instrumental commitment meant the entire network's legitimacy collapsed with a single donor's misconduct.
- Philanthropic commons funding: capital is retractable on the funder's schedule and accountable to the funder's metrics, making the funder's commitment conditional and reversible.

### 2. Embedding in the emerging order

**The test:** Are the patron's assets gaining or losing value as the emerging order advances?

If the patron's holdings appreciate as the new order consolidates, they are embedded in it. If their holdings depend on the old order persisting, they are embedded in the declining system.

**Positive anchors (embedded in the emerging order):**
- Carolingian prince-bishops (Alcuin, Hrabanus Maurus, Gerbert of Aurillac): held literate, institutional, and political assets anchored in the emerging Christian governance infrastructure.
- Florentine merchant-humanists: sat inside the new commercial order with liquid wealth and civic power they controlled. Their assets were denominated in banking, trade, and negotiable credit, all features of the emerging system.
- Abbasid secondary patron class (viziers, court physicians, merchants): professional and commercial interests created standing demand for the new knowledge. Their careers depended on the translation movement's outputs.

**Negative anchors (embedded in the declining order):**
- Late Roman senatorial aristocracy: vast landed estates and status denominated in the dying imperial order. As a class, they failed to reposition and were liquidated with the system they clung to.
- The Fuggers: wealth denominated in the indulgence economy and the Habsburg-papal financial system. Structurally committed to the order Luther attacked.
- Captured NGOs: survival dependent on the funding stream of the order they set out to change.

### 3. Protection during maximum exposure

**The test:** When the reform draws fire from the still-dominant order, can the patron absorb or deflect the attack without the reform being destroyed?

Reform generates organized opposition precisely when it starts to work. The patron who cannot provide cover at that moment cannot deliver durability.

**Positive anchors:**
- Lorenzo de' Medici sheltering Pico della Mirandola after the papal condemnation of the 900 Theses (1487). Lorenzo could protect Pico within Florence, though he could not overturn the papal ruling. Protection has a ceiling set by the tolerance of the still-dominant order.
- Charlemagne's imperial mandate (*Admonitio generalis*, 789) ordering every monastery and bishopric to run a school. The mandate carried the force of imperial authority during the period of maximum institutional vulnerability.
- The Hansa's collective boycott weapon: the ability to deny an entire market to an offending power (demonstrated at the Treaty of Stralsund, 1370).

**Negative anchors:**
- Roger Bacon: entire return structure routed through one pope. Clement IV's death in 1268 was fatal to the project. No constituency, no institutional carrier, no fallback.
- EA/FTX: no structural insulation between the movement's legitimacy and one donor's conduct. When FTX collapsed, the network's credibility collapsed with it.
- Francis Bacon: James I had no structural dependence on the reform's success and did not fund the institutional apparatus. The Royal Society was chartered 34 years after Bacon's death by a self-organizing community, on a timescale the reformer could not control.

## The replication mechanism (fourth criterion)

Durability is a property of the replication mechanism, not of funding volume. The reforms that outlived their patrons all carried a portable operating system that could reproduce without the founder. The Three-Feature Test's fourth criterion is therefore whether the project carries a durable replication mechanism, with four specific requirements:

**Portability.** The code can be instantiated in a new location without reference to a central headquarters. The Rule of St Benedict could be copied to any monastery. The *ius ubique docendi* made degrees portable across all of Christendom. Carolingian minuscule created a script legible from one region to another.

**No load-bearing central node.** The architecture survives the removal of any single node, including the founding node. The Benedictine network survived the fragmentation of the Carolingian empire after 843 because the code lived in every house. The Abbasid translation movement survived the end of caliphal patronage after 833 because the corpus had already propagated through dispersed copying and teaching.

**Constitutional or normative coordination layer.** Multi-scale coherence without central command requires a shared code paired with a periodic coordinating assembly. The Cistercian Charter of Charity (General Chapter, filiation, visitation). The Jesuit Constitutions. The Hanseatic Hansetag. The university system's shared curriculum, common language, and common degree structure.

**Immunity of the appointment/enforcement node from external capture.** The coherence mechanism is only as durable as its resistance to external capture of its local appointment and enforcement nodes. The Cistercian collapse came from the commendatory abbot system, in which external powers appointed non-Cistercian abbots for revenue, hollowing out local observance while the constitutional text survived unchanged. This is the single most transferable finding for any contemporary replication design.

## The Medici/Fugger spectrum

The master diagnostic in single-question form:

**Medici position (constitutive):** The patron's identity depends on the reform succeeding. Capital enters a structure that becomes the patron's own standing in the emerging order. The reform survives the patron's withdrawal because it has been embedded in a replicable medium.

**Fugger position (instrumental):** The patron funds reform as an allocation while their core position is committed to the declining order. The cultural patronage is decorative. When the transition arrives, the patron is on the side of the system being reformed.

The structural meaning of [[transvestment|transvestment]] is the move from the Fugger position to the Medici position (and, in the [[mycelial-patronage|Mycelial Patronage]] case, further, into the substrate itself). From instrumental funder to constitutive builder. From the Boethius path to the Cassiodorus path. The window for this repositioning is real and it closes.

## Failure-mode classification

The test's diagnostic power comes from the specificity of the failure modes it surfaces:

**Type 1: Single-patron fragility.** The return structure is routed through one person or one institution. When that node fails, the project fails. (Roger Bacon and Clement IV. EA and FTX.)

**Type 2: Instrumental commitment without constitutive embedding.** The patron funds reform as an allocation while their core position remains committed to the declining order. (Fuggers, ESG/impact capital, philanthropic commons funding.)

**Type 3: Programmatic vision without institutional carrier.** A comprehensive reform pitch exists, but no replication mechanism and no constitutively invested patron. (Francis Bacon; the Republic of Letters.)

**Type 4: Capture by the declining order.** The reform organization's survival becomes dependent on the funding stream of the order it set out to change. (NGOization; Compartamos; SKS Microfinance.)

**Type 5: Perception without timely repositioning.** The patron sees the transition coming and still fails to move assets and identity into the emerging order before the window closes. (Late Roman senatorial aristocracy.)

Each failure mode has a specific structural cause. The test surfaces which one applies.

## Applying the test

The test is not a scoring exercise. It is a structural diagnostic that surfaces where a proposed patron project is vulnerable and what would be required to strengthen it. A project failing the constitutive-investment test can be strengthened by embedding the patron more deeply. A project failing the embedding test can be strengthened by [[transvestment|transvestment]] into the emerging order. A project failing the protection test needs institutional insulation before it draws organized opposition. A project without a replication mechanism needs to build one before the founder is removed by time, distraction, or death.

The [[mycelial-patronage|Mycelial Patronage]] framework is the specific application of the test to the current bioregional transition. The two case studies developed in the corpus (Ibrahim Abouleish and SEKEM in one direction, the Bekker/Roos Babylonstoren portfolio in the other) test the diagnostic against real contemporary patronage.
