# Policy Alignments

This page documents the government policies, legislative frameworks, and international commitments that support the work the Valley of Grace BioHub and the Ward 2 Business Forum advise on. Each entry names the instrument, states its relevance, and maps it to one or more of the Forum's six advisory areas: Water and Catchment, Wildlife and Biodiversity, Heritage and Tourism, Food and Markets, Skills and Employment, and Education.

The purpose is transparency. Every activity the Forum advises on has a statutory or policy basis. This page is the reference.

---

## International

### Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (2022)

The UN Convention on Biological Diversity's post-2020 framework, adopted December 2022. South Africa is a signatory. Target 3 (the "30x30" target) commits parties to conserve 30% of terrestrial areas by 2030. Target 8 addresses climate adaptation through ecosystem-based approaches. Target 10 calls for biodiversity-positive management of agriculture, aquaculture, fisheries, and forestry. Target 19 requires substantial increase in financial resources for biodiversity.

**Forum areas:** Wildlife and Biodiversity; Water and Catchment.

### Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures (TNFD)

Voluntary framework for organisations to report and act on nature-related dependencies, impacts, risks, and opportunities. The JSE adopted TNFD-aligned disclosure guidance in 2025. Corporate ESG reporting creates a market for verified biodiversity data, which is the basis of the Forum's biodiversity data advisory.

**Forum areas:** Wildlife and Biodiversity.

### Paris Agreement and Nationally Determined Contributions

South Africa's updated NDC (2021, revised 2025) commits to a mitigation range with an adaptation component that includes water security, biodiversity, and land use. Catchment restoration and ecosystem-based adaptation in the Riviersonderend corridor align directly with the adaptation commitments.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment; Wildlife and Biodiversity.

## National

### Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996

Section 24 (environmental right): everyone has the right to an environment that is not harmful to health or wellbeing, and to have the environment protected through reasonable legislative and other measures that prevent ecological degradation, promote conservation, and secure ecologically sustainable development and use of natural resources while promoting justifiable economic and social development.

Section 25 (property clause): includes provisions on equitable access to land and tenure reform, directly relevant to the TRANCRAA process for Farm 39.

Schedules 4B and 5B assign local government functions that include municipal planning, water and sanitation services, local tourism, and local economic development.

**Forum areas:** All six.

### National Development Plan 2030 (NDP)

Chapter 5 (Environmental Sustainability): calls for a transition to an environmentally sustainable, climate-resilient, low-carbon economy and society. Names water security, biodiversity, land management, and ecosystem-based adaptation as priorities. Calls for expanded public employment programmes in environmental services.

Chapter 6 (Inclusive Rural Economy): envisions "vibrant, equitable, sustainable rural communities contributing towards food security for all" through successful land reform, job creation, and agricultural development. Names cooperatives and small-scale farming as instruments.

Chapter 3 (Economy and Employment): targets creation of 11 million jobs by 2030 through a more inclusive economy. Names the green economy, tourism, agro-processing, and the social economy as growth sectors.

**Forum areas:** All six. The NDP is the overarching national vision within which every other policy below operates.

### Economic Reconstruction and Recovery Plan (ERRP, 2020)

The Presidency's post-COVID recovery framework. Names green economy interventions, mass public employment in ecological infrastructure, and support for SMMEs and cooperatives as priority interventions. The Expanded Public Works Programme (EPWP) and the Presidential Employment Stimulus are the operational vehicles. Catchment restoration and biodiversity monitoring create EPWP-eligible employment.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment; Wildlife and Biodiversity; Skills and Employment.

### National Water Act 36 of 1998

Provides for the national water resource strategy, water allocation, the ecological reserve, and catchment management. Section 21 water use licences govern abstraction, storage, and stream flow reduction activities. The ecological reserve provision requires that enough water remains in rivers and wetlands to maintain ecological function before any allocation to human or economic use. Catchment management agencies (CMAs) are the intended implementation bodies. The Breede-Gouritz CMA covers the Riviersonderend corridor.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment.

### Mountain Catchment Areas Act 63 of 1970

Provides for the conservation, use, management, and control of land in mountain catchment areas. Proclaimed mountain catchment areas in the Riviersonderend corridor fall under CapeNature management. Fire management, alien clearing, and soil conservation provisions apply directly to the Forum's water yield advisory.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment.

### Zonderend Water Users Association (ZWUA)

Water Users Association established under Chapter 8 (section 98) of the National Water Act. The ZWUA operates in the Riviersonderend catchment and is the local institutional body responsible for collective water resource management among irrigators and other water users in the Zonderend system. Its stated vision is to be a "recognized inclusive body that promotes responsible and sustainable water management." Its mission includes restoring the Riviersonderend's water resources and supporting local employment through water stewardship. The ZWUA is a direct operational counterpart for the Forum's water and catchment advisory: its membership overlaps with the landowner base whose participation the water yield programme requires, and its institutional mandate aligns with the catchment restoration and alien clearing work the Forum advises on.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment; Food and Markets (irrigation and agricultural water allocation).

### Strategic Water Source Areas (SWSA)

Identified by the CSIR and SANBI through the National Freshwater Ecosystem Priority Areas (NFEPA) programme and the WRC's research programme. SWSAs supply a disproportionate quantity of South Africa's mean annual runoff. The Boland mountains and the upper Riviersonderend are designated SWSAs. Although no dedicated SWSA statute exists, WWF South Africa and others have advocated for formal legal protection, and the National Water Resource Strategy 2 and 3 reference SWSA protection as a national priority. The FR31PB bond programme is explicitly designed around the SWSA designation.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment.

### Climate Change Act 22 of 2024

Signed into law July 2024; proclaimed for implementation 2025. Establishes a national climate change response framework. Requires provinces and municipalities to include climate change considerations in their development plans. Mandates climate change risk and vulnerability assessments, adaptation plans, and mitigation reporting at municipal level. Section 22 requires that municipal IDPs and SDFs incorporate climate change response. Catchment restoration and ecosystem-based adaptation are named categories of adaptation response.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment; Wildlife and Biodiversity.

### National Environmental Management Act 107 of 1998 (NEMA)

The framework environmental legislation. Establishes co-operative governance principles, environmental impact assessment requirements, and the "polluter pays" principle. Relevant to any land use, development, or restoration activity the Forum advises on.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment; Wildlife and Biodiversity; Heritage and Tourism.

### National Environmental Management: Biodiversity Act 10 of 2004 (NEMBA)

Provides for biodiversity management plans, threatened species protection, and biodiversity stewardship. The biodiversity stewardship provisions are the legal basis for conservation agreements between landowners and conservation authorities (CapeNature in the Western Cape). These agreements are the instrument through which the leopard corridor monitoring and landowner stewardship programme operates.

**Forum areas:** Wildlife and Biodiversity.

### National Environmental Management: Protected Areas Act 57 of 2003 (NEMPAA)

Provides for the declaration and management of protected areas, including nature reserves, protected environments, and biodiversity stewardship sites. Landowner stewardship agreements in the Riviersonderend corridor may produce protected environments or contract nature reserves under this Act.

**Forum areas:** Wildlife and Biodiversity.

### National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (NBSAP 2026-2035)

Draft published for comment 5 August 2026 (deadline 4 September 2026). Aligns South Africa's biodiversity commitments with the Kunming-Montreal framework, though the terrestrial protection target of 21% by 2035 falls short of the framework's 30% by 2030 target. Other targets: 22% of degraded ecosystems under restoration, and a 10% increase in biodiversity economy contribution to GDP by 2035. The draft acknowledges a decline in real biodiversity expenditure from R14.31 billion (2016) to R12.9 billion (2024), underlining the gap between commitment and resourcing. Invasive species control is highlighted as a priority. The biodiversity economy concept (ecotourism, bioprospecting, wildlife ranching, and natural resource harvesting) treats conservation as an employment and enterprise sector, though the operational distinction between an economy that depends on biodiversity and an economy that restores biodiversity is not resolved in the draft.

**Forum areas:** Wildlife and Biodiversity; Skills and Employment; Water and Catchment (invasive species control).

### Transformation of Certain Rural Areas Act 94 of 1998 (TRANCRAA)

The statute governing the transfer of Farm 39 at Genadendal from state trust into a locally chosen receiving entity. See the dedicated [TRANCRAA](/research/trancraa) page for the full 27-year process history. Directly relevant to land tenure, community governance, and the receiving-entity question that underlies all coordination in Ward 2.

**Forum areas:** All six (tenure underpins every activity).

### Communal Property Associations Act 28 of 1996

Provides for the establishment of CPAs to hold property on behalf of communities. A CPA is currently being constituted as the receiving entity for Farm 39. The Act's governance requirements (constitution, general meetings, financial reporting) apply to any CPA that holds land in the valley.

**Forum areas:** Food and Markets (land access); Skills and Employment (governance capacity).

### Spatial Planning and Land Use Management Act 16 of 2013 (SPLUMA)

Replaced the fragmented apartheid-era planning legislation with a single national framework for spatial planning and land use management. Requires every municipality to adopt a Spatial Development Framework (SDF) and a land use scheme. Development principles include spatial justice, spatial sustainability, efficiency, and good administration. The SDF is the spatial expression of the IDP and must guide all land use decisions. Relevant to any development, change of land use, or zoning decision the Forum advises on.

**Forum areas:** Heritage and Tourism; Food and Markets; Education.

### National Heritage Resources Act 25 of 1999

Provides for the identification, assessment, and management of heritage resources. Section 34 protects structures older than 60 years. Section 38 requires heritage impact assessments for development affecting heritage sites. Genadendal's 1738 Moravian mission buildings and the 1838 teacher training college are protected heritage resources. The South African Heritage Resources Agency (SAHRA) and Heritage Western Cape are the implementing authorities. The Act is the statutory basis for the heritage tourism programme.

**Forum areas:** Heritage and Tourism; Education.

### Tourism Act 3 of 2014

Provides for the development and promotion of sustainable tourism. Establishes the South African Tourism Board and provides for provincial and municipal tourism structures. Requires that tourism development be inclusive and contribute to local economic development. The Leopard Corridor Destination Management Plan operates within this framework.

**Forum areas:** Heritage and Tourism.

### Companies Act 71 of 2008 (NPC provisions)

Provides for the formation of Non-Profit Companies. The NPC form is the recommended convening entity for the BioHub. Schedule 1 of the Act sets out the governance requirements for NPCs, including restrictions on distribution of assets and requirements for independent review.

**Forum areas:** Skills and Employment (organisational form).

### Co-operatives Act 14 of 2005

Provides for the formation, registration, and regulation of primary, secondary, and tertiary co-operatives. A primary cooperative requires five natural persons. The Ward 2 producers' cooperative and any federated cooperative structure operate under this Act. The Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) is the registrar.

**Forum areas:** Food and Markets.

### Co-operative Banks Act 40 of 2007

Provides for the establishment, registration, and regulation of co-operative banks and cooperative financial institutions (CFIs). The Co-operative Banks Development Agency (CBDA) within National Treasury supports the formation and supervision of CFIs. The ZA-FiCom pathway from stokvel to registered cooperative to cooperative bank follows this statutory ladder. A CFI is a cooperative that takes deposits and makes loans to its members, registered with the CBDA when it meets the prescribed thresholds.

**Forum areas:** Food and Markets (local finance); Skills and Employment (financial inclusion).

### Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, Section 30

Provides for Public Benefit Organisation (PBO) status. A PBO registered with SARS under Section 30 receives tax exemptions and can issue Section 18A certificates to donors. The NPC convening entity would apply for PBO status.

**Forum areas:** Skills and Employment (funding and governance).

### South African Schools Act 84 of 1996

Provides for a uniform system of public and independent schools. Section 45 provides for the registration of independent schools. An independent school must register with the provincial Head of Department (WCED in the Western Cape) and comply with minimum requirements for curriculum, governance, infrastructure, and staffing. The premium high school envisioned by the Forum would register under this Act.

**Forum areas:** Education.

### Continuing Education and Training Act 16 of 2006

Provides for Community Education and Training (CET) colleges, which replaced the former Adult Education and Training (AET) centres. CET colleges fall under the Department of Higher Education and Training. The Western Cape CET College operates through Community Learning Centres (CLCs) across the province. The adult learning centre envisioned by the Forum could operate as a CLC under the CET College, or as an independent registered training provider.

**Forum areas:** Education.

### National Qualifications Framework Act 67 of 2008

Provides for the NQF, the quality councils (Umalusi for general and further education, the QCTO for trades and occupations), and the recognition of qualifications. Any training programme the Learning Hub delivers that leads to a qualification or unit standard must be registered and quality-assured through the relevant SETA or quality council.

**Forum areas:** Education; Skills and Employment.

### Expanded Public Works Programme (EPWP)

Not a statute but a national programme anchored in the NDP and the Division of Revenue Act. Provides wage subsidies for labour-intensive public works, including environmental services (Working for Water, Working for Wetlands, Working on Fire, Working for Ecosystems). Alien clearing and catchment restoration in the Riviersonderend corridor are EPWP-eligible activities. The programme is a direct funding and employment channel for the Forum's water and catchment advisory.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment; Skills and Employment.

## Provincial (Western Cape)

### Western Cape Climate Change Response Strategy (Vision 2050)

The provincial climate change strategy, adopted 2014, revised 2022-2023. Identifies water security, biodiversity, food systems, and coastal management as priority adaptation areas. Names ecosystem-based adaptation (including catchment restoration, alien clearing, and ecological infrastructure investment) as a core adaptation pathway. The Overberg is identified as a climate vulnerability hotspot due to water stress and agricultural dependence.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment; Wildlife and Biodiversity; Food and Markets.

### Western Cape Green Economy Strategy Framework

Administered through GreenCape and the Department of Economic Development and Tourism (DEDAT). Identifies water, energy, waste, agriculture, biodiversity, and sustainable transport as green economy sectors. The 2025 Market Intelligence Reports identified a R25 billion investable opportunity in the Western Cape water sector. Catchment restoration, biodiversity monitoring, and regenerative agriculture are green economy activities under this framework.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment; Wildlife and Biodiversity; Food and Markets; Skills and Employment.

### Western Cape Biodiversity Spatial Plan

CapeNature's spatial plan identifying Critical Biodiversity Areas (CBAs) and Ecological Support Areas (ESAs) across the province. CBAs in the Riviersonderend corridor must be maintained in a natural state. The plan informs municipal SDFs and land use decisions. Any land use change the Forum advises on must be consistent with the biodiversity spatial plan.

**Forum areas:** Wildlife and Biodiversity; Heritage and Tourism.

### CapeNature Stewardship Programme

The provincial conservation authority's implementation of the biodiversity stewardship provisions under NEMBA and NEMPAA. Offers contract nature reserves, biodiversity agreements, and conservation areas as instruments for landowners to commit land to conservation. The leopard corridor landowner stewardship programme aligns directly with this programme.

**Forum areas:** Wildlife and Biodiversity.

### Protected Area Management Plan: Riviersonderend Mountain Catchment Area (RMCA PAMP)

The Riviersonderend Complex (approximately 28,580 hectares) comprises the Riviersonderend State Forest, Riviersonderend State Land, and Vrolijkheid Provincial Reserve. It forms part of the Cape Floristic Region Protected Areas World Heritage Site (extended 2015). The Final Draft PAMP (December 2020), prepared under NEMPAA, is administered by CapeNature. Management priorities include integrated invasive alien plant and fire management, catchment management (the mountain range is an important catchment for the Breede River system), and biodiversity monitoring. The PAMP acknowledges Genadendal and Greyton as surrounding towns and identifies environmental education with neighbours and local communities as a strategic objective (Strategy 5). The Forum's water and catchment advisory, leopard corridor monitoring, and educational programme all connect to the PAMP's stated management objectives.

**Forum areas:** Water and Catchment; Wildlife and Biodiversity; Education.

### Western Cape Provincial Spatial Development Framework (PSDF)

The provincial spatial framework guiding settlement patterns, land use, and infrastructure investment. Identifies rural nodes and their development potential. Greyton and Genadendal appear within the spatial policy guidance for the Overberg sub-region.

**Forum areas:** Heritage and Tourism; Food and Markets; Education.

### Wesgro Five-Year Strategic Plan 2026-2030

The Western Cape's official trade and investment promotion agency's strategy. Identifies green economy, agri-processing, tourism, and film as priority sectors for investment promotion. Wesgro is a potential institutional partner for positioning the valley's heritage tourism and agri-processing activities within the provincial investment pipeline.

**Forum areas:** Heritage and Tourism; Food and Markets.

## District (Overberg District Municipality, DC03)

### Overberg District IDP 2022/23-2026/27

The five-year integrated development plan for DC03. Identifies local economic development, tourism, climate change response, and environmental sustainability as strategic priorities. The Regional Economic Development Strategy and Recovery Plan (Annexure D) sets the district LED framework. The Overberg Climate Change and Biodiversity Forum convenes quarterly at district level.

**Forum areas:** All six.

### Overberg District LED and Tourism Strategy

Tourism coordination is a core district function. The district identifies agri-tourism, heritage tourism, eco-tourism, and adventure tourism as growth segments. The Overberg as a tourism destination is marketed through regional tourism structures. The Forum's heritage and tourism advisory aligns with the district's tourism coordination mandate.

**Forum areas:** Heritage and Tourism; Food and Markets.

### Overberg District Spatial Development Framework

The district SDF provides the spatial development guidance within which local municipal SDFs operate. Settlement hierarchy, infrastructure corridors, and development nodes are identified at district scale.

**Forum areas:** Heritage and Tourism; Food and Markets; Education.

## Municipal (Theewaterskloof Local Municipality, WC031)

### TWKM IDP 2022-2027 (2026 Amendment)

Approved by Council 28 May 2026. Names agriculture, tourism, agricultural product processing, and light industrial development as LED priority sectors. Greyton is described as a "retirement and holiday home base known for its village character." Genadendal's economy is described as relying on "development projects" and "small-scale agricultural production" with seasonal employment tied to surrounding fruit farms.

The IDP acknowledges the municipality's narrow tax base and, since 2025, the Provincial Financial Recovery Plan constraining discretionary spending. The 2026 Amendment reflects the Section 139(5)(a) intervention.

**Forum areas:** All six.

### TWKM Spatial Development Framework

The municipal SDF under SPLUMA. Governs land use zoning, settlement edges, and development applications for Greyton and Genadendal. Any change of land use the Forum advises on (tourism facilities, market infrastructure, educational buildings) must comply with the SDF and obtain municipal land use approvals.

**Forum areas:** Heritage and Tourism; Food and Markets; Education.

### TWKM LED Strategy

Embedded within the IDP. Sectoral focus on agriculture, tourism, agri-processing, and the green economy. Identifies conservancies (Klein Swartberg, Groenlandberg, Theewaterskloof, Akkedisberg) as environmental assets. The LED strategy is the municipal instrument the Forum's advisory work connects to most directly.

**Forum areas:** All six.

### TWKM Section 139(5)(a) Financial Recovery Plan (2024/2025)

Provincial intervention under the Constitution's Section 139(5)(a). Triggered by irregular expenditure of R353 million and cash reserves falling from R85 million to R2.1 million. Genadendal accounts for approximately 20% of outstanding municipal debt. The recovery plan constrains the municipality's operating capacity and is directly relevant to any expectation that TWKM will fund infrastructure or services in Ward 2. The Forum's economic logic (anchored in the water bond and community savings structure rather than municipal budgets) is designed to work within this constraint.

**Forum areas:** All six.
