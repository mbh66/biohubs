# Genadendal Transformation Committee (TC / GTC)

The elected representative body of the Farm 39 beneficiaries under the [TRANCRAA](/trancraa) transformation process. Formed on 9 November 2004 as the Genadendal Transformation Committee (TC) with 27 representatives from organizations and sectors, its constitution was adopted on 16 November 2004. The committee is the beneficiary counterparty to [TWKM](/entities/twkm) and to the Department of Rural Development and Land Reform (DRDLR) in the transformation process. From 2014 the same or a successor body is referenced in the record as the Genadendal Trust Committee (GTC); the exact relationship between the 2004 TC and the 2014-onward GTC is documented below as an open question.

## Formation and mandate

The 2004 TC was elected as the beneficiary body for the TRANCRAA process for Farm 39, following the formal start of that process by TWKM's tender for transformation consultants in March 2002 and the appointment of Urban Dynamics Western Cape (UDWC) in November 2002. TRANCRAA's design requires community participation in the choice of receiving entity, and the TC was the vehicle through which the community was to exercise that participation.

The TC's mandate covered representation of beneficiaries as defined by TRANCRAA's qualifying date of 2 November 1998 (persons normally resident on that date, or liable for rates and levies), engagement with the appointed consultants, negotiation with the municipality and the national department, and eventual selection of the receiving entity to which Farm 39 would transfer.

Its 27-member composition drew from organizations and sectors within Genadendal, giving the body its representative character. The constitution adopted on 16 November 2004 formalized its internal governance.

## Actions in the transformation process

The following summary draws on the [Significant Dates](/significant-dates) timeline. Every event carries the IC (Independently Corroborated) marker at source.

**Legal engagement (2005 to 2006).** In December 2005 the TC appointed Regan Brown Attorneys, requesting a halt to land sales, full budget disclosure, and a pause on UDWC involvement. On 6 June 2006 Regan Brown formally declared a dispute, primarily on the tender process for the appointment of consultants.

**High Court application (2007).** On 31 January 2007 the TC filed a High Court application (Case 926/2007) seeking judicial review of TWKM's administrative actions: the appointment of UDWC, payment of UDWC accounts, and sale and transfer of Trust Land to outsiders.

**Mediation and settlement (2007 to 2008).** Pre-mediation was held in June 2007. Formal mediation at Houw Hoek followed on 27-28 September 2007. The Houw Hoek Agreement of Settlement was concluded on 17 October 2008: no distinction between binnemeent and buitemeent; moratorium on Trust Land sales; residents list to be updated; new Steering Committee. The Agreement was made an order of the Cape High Court on 22 October 2008 by Justice Yekiso. DLA and the TC signed the Agreement promptly following TWKM's October 2008 signature.

**Exclusion and rejection (2010).** In April 2010 the TC rejected the appointment of a new facilitator. On 27 June 2010 a community meeting at Emil Weder School Hall (organized outside the TWKM-driven process) rejected the new facilitator, the misrepresentation of Farm 39's scope, and the meent distinction. On 7 July 2010 TWKM, DRDLR, and UDWC met without the TC and decided the Agreement "cannot be implemented and should not be revised. No further public participation, no referendum, no updating of residents list." From this point the TC was structurally excluded from the process by the other parties' unilateral decision, while the court order the TC had won remained legally operative.

**Land Claims Court filing (2014).** On 10 October 2014 the Genadendal Trust Committee (GTC) filed at the Land Claims Court (LCC64/2014), seeking an interdict against TWKM from alienating, encumbering, or diminishing any portion of Farm 39.

**Institutional realignment (2025).** In 2025 the GTC aligned with AfriForum and Kaapse Forum. A Communal Property Association is being constituted as the receiving entity for Farm 39.

## TC to GTC: an open question

The 2004 formation record names the "Genadendal Transformation Committee (TC)." The 2014 and 2025 records name the "GTC" (Genadendal Trust Committee). Three possibilities are consistent with the record on the current wiki:

1. **Continuous identity, name change.** The 2004 TC continued in operation and adopted or was rendered by observers as "Genadendal Trust Committee" from some point after the 2010 process collapse.
2. **Successor body.** After the 2010 collapse the TC was formally succeeded by a new body constituted as the GTC, carrying forward the beneficiary representation role.
3. **Parallel bodies.** The TC and GTC are related but distinct entities that coexisted for some period.

Which of these is correct affects the legal position of the LCC64/2014 filing (whether it was brought by the original TRANCRAA beneficiary body or by a successor with a separate legal personality) and the relationship of the 2025 institutional alignments to the earlier committee's decisions. This question is flagged for verification.

## 2025 alignments and the CPA question

The 2025 alignment of the GTC with AfriForum and Kaapse Forum is a new institutional positioning in the record. Neither party's specific role in the Farm 39 transformation is documented on the wiki. The CPA being constituted as the receiving entity for Farm 39 is a distinct legal form under the Communal Property Associations Act 28 of 1996, and its constitution, membership qualification rules, and relationship to the GTC and to the earlier TC beneficiary list are all open questions.

The identity of the receiving entity is the central open decision in the TRANCRAA process. Whether the CPA now being constituted meets the participation requirements of TRANCRAA, whether its membership tracks the 2 November 1998 beneficiary qualification, and whether its constitution reflects the coordination architecture the community would design for itself are the substantive questions for the next phase.
