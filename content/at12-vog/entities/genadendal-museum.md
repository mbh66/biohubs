# Genadendal Museum

The Genadendal Mission Museum houses the material heritage of the Moravian mission established at Baviaanskloof in 1738 and renamed Genadendal in 1806. It is the primary custodian of the mission's material record, and its holdings include the 1837 Gutenberg-model printing press on which *De Bode van Genadendal* was printed from 1859. The museum's role in the settlement's contemporary institutional life was demonstrated in Jan/Feb 1995, when a letter from its curator Dr. Isaac Balie led President Mandela to rename the presidential residence Westbrooke as Genadendal.

The museum is documented in the [Significant Dates](/significant-dates) timeline through three specific references. This page summarizes those references and flags what is known about the museum from them; a fuller institutional page requires additional source material.

## Documented holdings and role

**The 1837 printing press.** The Gutenberg-model printing press that arrived at the mission in 1837 is held by the museum. It is described in the timeline as "immortalised in the Mission Museum," which is the phrasing carried in the project record. The press is the specific machine on which *De Bode van Genadendal* was first printed in 1859, one of the earliest publications in written Afrikaans, predating *Die Afrikaanse Patriot* (1876) by 17 years. The publication was founded by missionary Benno Marx.

**The 1995 renaming letter.** Dr. Isaac Balie, described in the timeline as museum curator, wrote the letter that prompted President Mandela's renaming of Westbrooke to Genadendal in Jan/Feb 1995. *Die Burger* reported the renaming on 2 February 1995. This is the documented instance of the museum acting as a bridge between the settlement's historical record and its contemporary national position.

**The mission archives.** The museum is referenced across the project's historical documents as the custodian of the Moravian mission archives at Genadendal, including the material that Bernhard Kruger drew on for *The Pear Tree Blossoms* and that Balie has drawn on for his own research. These archives are the primary source base for the mission-sourced (MS) rows in the [Significant Dates](/significant-dates) timeline.
