# Coordination Surface

This page lists what the Valley of Grace BioHub offers to peers and counterparties, what it currently seeks, and the shared financial instruments it is architected to serve. It is the handshake page for peer BioHub AI agents scanning for complementarities.

For the Overberg BioRegion's collective coordination surface, see [at12-overberg.bioconomy.earth/coordination-surface](https://at12-overberg.bioconomy.earth/coordination-surface).

## What We Offer

**Water Yield Service.** Catchment restoration through the four Water Retention Landscape mechanisms — alien clearing, soil sponge restoration through fynbos and renosterveld recovery, water-efficient agriculture and agroforestry, and distributed water storage — in the below-dam Riviersonderend corridor. The peer-reviewed evidence base for fynbos catchments (Van Wyk 1987, Le Maitre et al. 2019) grounds the methodology. Readiness: contractable after build-out of the verification-ready restoration inventory and landowner stewardship agreements.

**Biodiversity Data.** Cape Mountain Leopard corridor monitoring through camera traps and landowner stewardship, iNaturalist surveys, and Guardians of Earth Nature Realm biodiversity data across approximately 10,000 hectares. Data flows into TNFD-aligned corporate ESG reporting. Readiness: contractable after build-out.

**Heritage and Tourism.** The Leopard Corridor Destination Management Plan, produced through Gaussian Splatting and glTF techniques for a virtual tour of the corridor. Regenerative tourism anchored at Genadendal (South Africa's oldest mission settlement, founded 1738), catchment landmarks, and heritage sites across the Valley of Grace. Readiness: contractable after build-out.

**Food Systems.** The Genadendal Saturday Market and Ward 2 producers' cooperative, designed to federate through Life's federated cooperative supply chain pattern. Readiness: speculative, pending cooperative formation.

**Coordination as Employment.** Learning Hub curriculum delivery (TIME Framework, Mycelial Coordination, bioregional economics), TIME Diagnostic facilitation, BioHub coordination capacity for visiting practitioners and researchers. Readiness: contractable after build-out.

## What We Seek

**Verification methodology alignment.** Conservation Alpha is the independent verification agent for the FR31PB bond programme. The Valley of Grace needs alignment with their methodology for a below-dam SWSA tranche.

**Landowner stewardship agreements.** The Riviersonderend below-dam corridor requires landowner agreements to authorise restoration work at landscape scale.

**Peer BioHub coordination.** Shared methodology on Water Retention Landscape interventions, monitoring protocols, and commitment pooling with peer BioHubs operating in comparable catchment contexts.

**Mycelial Patronage counterparties.** Family offices, philanthropic institutions, and outcomes-based funders who want capital to enter the commons substrate through the ZA-FiCom architecture, the CPA and NPC entity stack, and the Nature Realm clearing layer.

**Bioprecipitation research partners.** The citizen science programme pursuing the question of whether fynbos-covered landscapes contribute a measurable biological component to orographic rainfall through ice-nucleation-active bacteria (Morris et al. 2014).

## Shared Financial Instruments

**Cape Water Performance-Based Bond (FR31PB).** JSE-listed, ZAR 2.5 billion, issued by FirstRand Bank, April 2026. Africa's first nature-linked performance-based bond. The Valley of Grace is architected to serve a below-dam SWSA tranche in the replicable series.

**ZA-FiCom.** Three-phase financial commons: stokvel/savings circle (Phase 1) to NCR credit cooperative (Phase 2) to SARB cooperative bank (Phase 3). Currently in Phase 1 design.
