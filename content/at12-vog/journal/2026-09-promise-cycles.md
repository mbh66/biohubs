## Context

The brief was written as a companion to the [Promise Theory Across TIMN](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/research/promise-theory-across-timn) research brief published on the BioConomy wiki on 5 September 2026. That brief supplies the general diagnostic: Burgess's promise/imposition distinction mapped onto Ronfeldt's four cumulative TIMN forms. This brief applies the diagnostic to the Valley of Grace Significant Dates timeline and shows the promise-to-imposition arc repeated four times in one geographic community between 1737 and 2026.

The structural argument in one line: each cycle opened with a bounded promise that incumbents expanded past scope of authority, holding coordination together by imposition once the gap between promise and delivery opened. The cumulative substrate has made grant-recipient status the cheapest available behavior for the current generation. The 2020 COVID feeding scheme is the controlled test: when the substrate briefly opened a coordination position that rewarded contribution, the community produced.

## Analytical Purpose

The brief supplies the historical grounding for the BioHub's positioning inside the +E form. Two structural claims from the piece feed downstream coordination work.

The 1858 "in trust" grant language on Farm 39 is the seed of every subsequent cycle's imposition mechanism, and the 2010 collapse of the Houw Hoek Agreement is the terminal event of Cycle 4. A close legal reading of the 1858 grant against period Roman-Dutch and English trust doctrine is flagged as follow-up.

The 1927 Glebe Lands (68 hectares in Genadendal and Bereaville, excluded from TRANCRAA) carry a title predating the coercion continuum's reach into the valley. They are a candidate substrate anchor for the +E instruments the BioHub is designing. Speculative Projection until verified with the Moravian Church's own governance position.
